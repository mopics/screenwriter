Around 0 AD, the ships of Gades acted as the primary maritime bridge connecting the wild Atlantic frontier with the wealthy heart of the Roman Mediterranean.
The trade routes mapped below highlight how Gades served as a massive commercial funnel for the Roman Empire:

## 🏛️ 1. The Rome Express (The Main Arterial Route)
This was the most lucrative and heavily traveled route. Gades shipped the premium resources of southern Hispania directly to Ostia, the main port of Rome.

* The Cargo: Thousands of tons of premium garum sociorum, liquid silver from the Tharsis mines, Baetican olive oil, and high-quality wool.
* The Route: Ships sailed east through the Strait of Gibraltar, hugged the coast of Ibiza, crossed the open Balearic Sea to the western tip of Sardinia, and made a straight run to the mouth of the Tiber River.
* The Timeline: With favorable winds, this 1,500-mile journey took roughly 7 to 10 days.

## 🇬🇧 2. The Northern Atlantic Route (The Tin Route)
Long before Rome arrived, Gades controlled the secret sea lanes heading north into the treacherous Bay of Biscay and the English Channel.

* The Cargo: The primary target was tin from Cornwall (Britain) and northwestern Hispania (Galicia), which was absolutely vital for making bronze. They also brought back British lead, hunting dogs, and hides.
* The Route: Ships battled heavy Atlantic swells moving north along the coast of modern Portugal, rounded Cape Finisterre, and crossed the open sea directly to Brittany (France) and the southern coast of Britain.
* The Impact: This route was so dangerous that only the specialized, deep-hulled Gaditan vessels could reliably survive it.

## 🦁 3. The West African Route (The Exotic Route)
Building on ancient Phoenician exploration maps, traders from Gades pushed south past the Pillars of Hercules along the Atlantic coast of Africa.

* The Cargo: Gold dust, ivory, purple dye extracted from Atlantic murex snails, and exotic wild animals for the Roman gladiatorial amphitheaters. They also established seasonal fishing outposts to catch high-fat tuna.
* The Route: Vessels sailed southwest along the coast of modern Morocco, stopping at trading posts like Lixus and Mogador (Essaouira), sometimes venturing as far south as the Canary Islands.

## 🪵 4. The Overland Route: The Via Augusta
While Gades was a maritime superpower, it was also the starting point of the Via Augusta, the longest Roman road in Hispania.

* The Cargo: High-value, low-weight goods like gold coins, official Roman mail, and luxury textiles.
* The Route: This road connected Gades directly to Rome by land, running up through Seville, Córdoba, Valencia, Barcelona, across the Pyrenees, through southern Gaul, and down into Italy.
